# Shop self-registration

Self-service setup extends the existing atomic business onboarding transaction. Actor's public entry offers Create your shop; standalone partner sign-in links to it. Owners enter business/contact/location details and a password, then sign in normally. Public registration creates only a partner identity, its organization and location, and disabled network controls. No staff invitations, verified business ownership/contact, capacity, capabilities or qualification evidence are manufactured. Contact verification, account recovery, storefront publication, service menus and staff management remain future slices.

`POST /api/shops/register` is public, strict and IP rate limited to five attempts/hour per Core instance. It accepts requestId, businessName, operatorName, email, password and location (name, address, city, region, countryCode). It does not accept role, organization IDs or readiness controls. Atomicity, password hashing, email uniqueness and request-key serialization are shared with Ops onboarding. Public retries must match the original source, normalized details and password; altered credentials or Ops-created request IDs cannot be replayed through this entry point. Identical in-page retries preserve the key; editing details generates a new one. Credentials remain in component memory, never browser persistent storage. Page refresh requires re-entry; an already created owner can sign in normally.

The audit transaction attributes self-registration to the new partner identity and actor, with shop_self_registration provenance. Ops-created records retain their administrator attribution and source. Both appear in Ops onboarding lists. A new account remains configuration_required, routing_enabled=false, accepts_overflow=false and under managed business-service policy. Existing authorization/qualification gates continue to control restricted actions. Registration is not business/issuer verification. Existing shop scope derives from authenticated actor organization/location and prevents access to other shops' intake and repair orders.

Database acceptance covers public creation/login and walk-in intake, no fabricated readiness, audit attribution, concurrent retries, source conflicts, rollback on duplicate email, shop isolation, privileged payload rejection and IP limiting. Frontend checks cover Actor and partner builds. Rate limiting uses the existing in-memory Core limiter; distributed abuse prevention/contact verification should be added before unrestricted mass acquisition.
